Communications portfolio

Seven pieces of my own work, each one live on the web. They show how I write in plain language, build pages that everyone can use, make stories for children and families, and turn public data into something a person can read.

Piece 1

Accessibility controls

What I did: I built a settings page where a reader chooses text size, line spacing, colour-vision modes and colour themes, and every page of the site follows those choices. The site is tested against the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 level AA, with automatic checks on every change.

Why it matters here: Health information has to reach every family, including people who read with low vision or colour blindness.

Piece 2

Plain-language writing with my byline

What I did: I wrote “How We Built It,” a series of thirteen posts for the Chattanooga.Digital blog that explains, for people who are not developers, why and how a community group runs its own online tools. Part 0 is the place to start. Every post is signed “By Jonathan Pohlner.”

Why it matters here: It is a bylined writing sample that takes a hard topic and explains it step by step.

Read part 0 of the series

Piece 3

The House that Code Built

What I did: I drew an illustrated house in layers. Each chapter switches on another layer, so a reader watches the house get built the same way a web page is built.

Why it matters here: It is my illustration and graphic design, used to explain a technical subject with pictures instead of jargon.

Open the house

Piece 4

HatCoatAndBoots, a children's book

What I did: I made a children's book about natural building in three chapters, Hat, Coat and Boots, for reading on a screen. The reader can make the text larger and switch the colours to suit their eyes.

Why it matters here: It shows a story for children that is also built for accessible reading.

Open the book

Piece 5

A music and stage-lights game for kids

What I did: I made a game, built on Base44, that plays in the browser. A band of seven robot musicians performs on an outdoor stage, and the player mixes the band, changes the tempo and works the lights and cameras. The light board is Shelly Evans’s work.

Why it matters here: It is something a child and a parent can open together and play. It needs no sign-in and no account.

Play the game

Piece 6

A site that guides families through steps

What I did: This pet-adoption site is built on the starting code from my web studio, ScriptHammer, and I run its code reviews. It explains how adopting works in three plain steps and shows an applicant where their application stands.

Why it matters here: It is the same job a family-facing health page has: say what happens next, in order, in words anyone can follow.

Open the adoption site

Piece 7

Chattanooga in 3D

What I did: I turned public map and laser-survey data into an open 3D atlas of 13,877 buildings in Chattanooga that anyone can explore in a web browser.

Why it matters here: It shows I can take a large set of public numbers and present it so a reader sees it at once.
